Frequently asked questions

How do I cancel my subscription or free trial?

Subscriptions are handled by Apple, so cancelling happens in your Apple Account:

  1. Open Settings on your iPhone
  2. Tap your name at the top
  3. Tap Subscriptions
  4. Select ShyBreak and tap Cancel Subscription

You can also reach this from inside the app: Settings → Manage subscription. Cancel at least 24 hours before your renewal date to avoid being charged for the next period.

I subscribed but the app still says it's locked

Open the app and go to Settings → Restore purchases. Make sure you're signed in with the same Apple Account you used to buy it. If it still doesn't unlock, email me and I'll sort it out.

I changed phones. Where's my progress?

ShyBreak stores everything on your device and nothing on a server, which means nobody — including me — can read what you write. The trade-off is that your history doesn't move between phones. Your subscription does: use Restore purchases on the new device.

Today's challenge doesn't fit my day

Tap Can't do this one — swap it under the challenge. You get one swap a day. If challenges keep assuming a workplace or friends you don't have, update Settings → Who's around you and the app will stop giving you those.

The challenges are too hard, or too easy

Two places to fix that. Settings → Current level lets you move up or down yourself. Settings → Your ladder lets you re-rate how hard each situation feels — worth doing every few weeks, since things that were hard in month one are often easier by month three.

How do I delete my data?

Settings → Start over erases everything on the device. Deleting the app does the same. Since nothing is stored on a server, there's nothing left behind afterwards.

Can I use it without a subscription?

The app is free to download, and you can go through setup and see your plan. Daily challenges need a subscription. The yearly plan includes a 3-day free trial, so you can try everything at no cost.

When reps aren't enough

ShyBreak is training, not treatment. It can't diagnose anything and it doesn't replace talking to someone qualified.

Any of these is worth a conversation with a doctor or a therapist:

  • The same situations feel just as hard after weeks of practice.
  • You're avoiding more than you used to, not less.
  • It's affecting your work, your studies, or your relationships.
  • You're carrying something heavier than nerves.
